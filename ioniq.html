📜 A Brief History

The Ioniq was Hyundai’s first dedicated eco-friendly model line

🚙 Ioniq Hybrid (HEV)

2016

Launched as a 2017 model. First dedicated hybrid from Hyundai with a 1.6L GDI Atkinson-cycle engine and 32 kW electric motor. Set new standards for aerodynamic efficiency (Cd 0.24).

2019

Facelift: redesigned bumpers, LED headlights, larger 10.25-inch navigation screen, digital instrument cluster option, new steering wheel.

2022

Production ends. The Ioniq nameplate transitions to the Ioniq 5 & Ioniq 6 dedicated BEV lineup.

🔌 Ioniq Plug-in Hybrid (PHEV)

2017

Introduced one year after the HEV. Same 1.6L engine but with a larger 44.5 kW electric motor and 8.9 kWh battery pack. Total system output: 165 PS.

2019

Facelift aligned with the HEV — updated styling, improved EV range (up to 63 km WLTP), Bluelink app with scheduled charging.

2022

Production ends alongside the HEV. Both models sold over 250,000 units combined worldwide.

🛋️ Interior & Technology

Minimalist, tech-forward cabin with subtle differences

Hybrid (HEV) Interior

  • ✅ 7-inch touchscreen (10.25-inch after 2019 facelift)
  • ✅ Wireless phone charger
  • ✅ Apple CarPlay / Android Auto
  • ✅ Button-type shift-by-wire (D, N, R, P)
  • ✅ Heated front seats, smart cruise control

Plug-in Hybrid (PHEV) Interior

  • ✅ All HEV features, plus:
  • ⚡ EV-specific instrument cluster with range display
  • ⚡ EV/HEV drive mode selector button
  • ⚡ Bluelink app — scheduled charging
  • ⚡ Charge status & energy flow display

💡 Key Interior Difference

The PHEV adds an EV/HEV mode selector on the centre console. Press it to force all-electric driving and save the battery for city zones. The HEV cannot drive purely on electric for meaningful distances — the small 1.56 kWh battery only assists the engine.

⚡ Performance & Driving

Power, acceleration, and the feel behind the wheel

HEV Driving Experience

  • 🚀 0–100: 10.8–11.1 seconds
  • ⚖️ Lighter: 1,410–1,440 kg
  • 🔇 Seamless hybrid transitions — barely notice the engine cut in
  • 🔄 Regenerative braking via paddle shifters
  • 🛣️ Sport mode livens up the DCT

PHEV Driving Experience

  • 🚀 0–100: 10.2–10.6 seconds
  • ⚖️ Heavier: 1,530–1,570 kg (+120 kg battery)
  • 🔇 Genuinely silent in EV mode up to 120 km/h
  • ⚡ Stronger electric punch off the line (44.5 kW motor)
  • 🔋 EV button — force electric-only in city zones

✅ Pros & Cons

Which one fits YOUR lifestyle?

🔵 Ioniq Hybrid (HEV)

✅ PROS

  • 👍 No plug, no hassle — fill up and go
  • 👍 Bigger boot: 455 litres
  • 👍 Lighter = more agile handling
  • 👍 Lower purchase price
  • 👍 Still excellent 3.4 L/100km
  • 👍 8-year battery warranty

❌ CONS

  • 👎 No meaningful electric-only range
  • 👎 Less powerful (141 PS)
  • 👎 Higher CO₂ than PHEV

🟢 Ioniq Plug-in (PHEV)

✅ PROS

  • 👍 52–63 km EV range — commute on electricity
  • 👍 1.1 L/100km if charged regularly
  • 👍 More powerful: 165 PS
  • 👍 Faster 0–100: 10.2 s
  • 👍 Lowest CO₂: 26 g/km
  • 👍 Tax & city incentives in many EU countries

❌ CONS

  • 👎 Smaller boot: 341 litres
  • 👎 Heavier = less agile
  • 👎 Need access to charging
  • 👎 Higher purchase price
  • 👎 2h 15min charge time

🏆 The Verdict

Which Ioniq should you choose?

🔵

Choose the HEV if…

  • ✅ You don’t have easy access to charging
  • ✅ You need maximum boot space
  • ✅ You want the lowest upfront cost
  • ✅ You drive mostly highway / long distances
  • ✅ You want simplicity — just drive it
🟢

Choose the PHEV if…

  • ✅ You can charge at home or work
  • ✅ Your daily commute is under 50 km
  • ✅ You want the lowest running costs
  • ✅ You qualify for EV tax incentives
  • ✅ You want that silent EV driving experience

🏁 Final Word

Both the Ioniq Hybrid and Ioniq Plug-in Hybrid are exceptional cars that punch above their weight class. The HEV is the practical, no-compromise choice — excellent fuel economy without any lifestyle changes. The PHEV is for those who can plug in — offering genuine electric commuting with the safety net of a petrol engine for longer trips. Neither is wrong — it depends on your driving.
